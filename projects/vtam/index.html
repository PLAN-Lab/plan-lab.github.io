Abstract

Tactile vision-language-action models (VLAs) and tactile world-action models (WAMs) are a promising new paradigm for contact-rich manipulation, but existing models either underutilize touch, rely on large-scale tactile pretraining, or incur high inference latency due to iterative video generation. We present VTAM, an efficient visuo-tactile world-action model for contact-rich manipulation that requires no tactile or action pretraining. RGB and tactile observations share a frozen video VAE and the pretrained video transformer weights. VTAM first adapts the video backbone to jointly predict future visual and tactile latents, then fine-tunes it end-to-end with a randomly initialized action expert that predicts actions, proprioceptive states, and a light flow-force proxy computed from tactile optical flow. Trained only on task demonstrations, VTAM outperforms all baselines on four real-robot tasks across two platforms with different tactile sensors, outperforms all baselines without tactile pretraining in average success on eight UniVTAC simulation tasks, and is comparable to tactile VLAs and WAMs pretrained on over 30K hours of largely visuo-tactile robot data (83.4% vs. 84.5% for N0-TWAM). Without full video denoising at inference, VTAM is approximately 6× and 16× faster than N0-TWAM and LingBot-VA without compilation, with compiled latency comparable to Fast-WAM. Ablations show that feeding frozen-VAE tactile features directly to the action expert, bypassing tactile world modeling, fails on every tested task, that Stage I visuo-tactile world-model fine-tuning improves UniVTAC success on most tasks, and that flow-force proxy keeps the policy from fully ignoring touch. The tactile encoder transfers zero-shot across GelSight Mini and Xense Photon sensors.

Contributions

  • Tactile world modeling without tactile pretraining. VTAM couples self-supervised tactile world modeling with action learning, transferring a pretrained video world model to touch with only task demonstrations: no tactile or action pretraining, and no video generation at inference.
  • A light flow-force proxy. Computed directly from tactile images and predicted by the action expert, it encourages tactile use during action learning without an external force sensor.
  • Real-robot and simulation evaluation. Four real-robot tasks on two platforms (xArm6 with GelSight Mini; Flexiv Rizon 4 with Xense Photon) and eight UniVTAC tasks show data efficiency, competitive performance and inference speed, and zero-shot cross-sensor tactile encoding.

VTAM Method

RGB and tactile observations share a frozen video VAE and the pretrained video transformer weights. A visuo-tactile world model predicts future visual and tactile latents; in a single forward pass at inference, its predictive latents are injected into a flow-matching action expert that generates actions without generating video.

  1. Stage I · Visuo-tactile world-model fine-tuning

    Task-specific fine-tuning extends the pretrained video model to jointly predict future visual and tactile latents from about 100 demonstrations per task, without any tactile pretraining.

  2. Stage II · Action learning

    A randomly initialized flow-matching action expert, trained end-to-end with the backbone, predicts actions, proprioceptive states, and a light flow-force proxy computed from tactile optical flow, which keeps the policy from ignoring touch.
