Abstract

Generative simulation can automate robot environment construction, but physically valid environments are not necessarily useful for training. The challenge is to identify environments that address a policy’s failures and supervision that helps it overcome them. We introduce CoDA, a framework in which a simulation data engine and an action policy co-evolve through a shared heterogeneous memory of environment construction, policy failures, and learning progress. The memory compiles validated diagnoses from a vision-language critic into generation programs that recreate difficulties and training programs that help the policy overcome them. We further propose Text2potential to convert these diagnoses into staged potentials for action-chunk value learning, providing dense supervision for policy updates while preserving the task’s optimal policy under standard shaping assumptions. Measured learning progress guides further generation and program refinement. Across four manipulation task families, CoDA improves out-of-distribution success from 5.3% after supervised fine-tuning to 27.6%, exceeding the strongest closed-loop baseline by 9.5 percentage points with the same generation budget. On LIBERO-Pro, 15 rounds of generation and training raise success from 25% to 84% under position perturbations and from 1% to 76% under instruction perturbations.

Contributions

  • Closed-loop co-evolution. CoDA co-evolves a simulation data engine and an action policy. Each round of environment synthesis is guided by the policy’s measured learning progress, rather than scene validity or current task success alone.
  • Shared heterogeneous memory. A memory shared by the generator and the policy combines episodic evidence, semantic difficulty modes, and procedural programs. The same diagnosis compiles into a generation program that recreates the difficulty and a training program that helps the policy overcome it, both indexed by difficulty mode and reusable across tasks.
  • Dense supervision through Text2potential. Text2potential compiles failure diagnoses into simulator-state potentials that shape action-chunk value targets. Only chunk endpoints are needed. With a fixed, bounded potential and appropriate terminal handling, shaping preserves the policies that maximize the original task return.
  • Improved out-of-distribution generalization. Across four manipulation task families, CoDA achieves 27.6% OOD success versus 5.3% after supervised fine-tuning, exceeding success-rate-guided generation by 9.5 percentage points with the same budget. On LIBERO-Pro, success rises from 25% to 84% on position generalization and from 1% to 76% on task generalization.

Build practice that targets a difficulty.

The data engine writes executable scenes with independently editable task, spatial, and object-attribute factors. Every scene passes validity checks before the policy tries it.

Generation programsRecreate the conditions the policy needs to practice.
02 / 05 · Diagnose

Ask why an attempt failed.

A vision–language critic links a failure to a scene factor and a phase of the trajectory. Targeted edits and control batches test whether that diagnosis holds.

Grounded in rollout evidenceA diagnosis is validated before it becomes a reusable program.
03 / 05 · Remember

Connect experience to reusable knowledge.

Episodic evidence becomes semantic difficulty modes. Procedural memory stores the paired programs that guide both generation and training.

One diagnosis → two programsRecreate the difficulty. Provide supervision to overcome it.
04 / 05 · Train

Turn a diagnosis into a learning signal.

Text2potential combines completed subgoals with progress on the next one. The resulting potential shapes chunk-level value targets; estimated advantages then weight the policy’s supervised updates.

Text2potentialDense training feedback; no potential or value critic is needed at deployment.
05 / 05 · Measure

Let learning progress close the loop.

CoDA measures improvement on each difficulty mode after training. Progress and success rate guide where to spend the next round’s generation budget.

Text2potential: the two key equations +

From subgoals to dense feedback

A validated diagnosis is compiled into cumulative completion predicates and progress functions. The potential counts completed subgoals and adds progress on the next one.

Staged potential · Eq. (2)
k*(s)=∑k=1mdonek(s)Φ(s)={k*(s)+φk*(s)+1(s)k*(s)<mmk*(s)=mk^*(s)=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\mathrm{done}_k(s),\qquad \Phi(s)=\begin{cases}k^*(s)+\varphi_{k^*(s)+1}(s),&k^*(s)<m,\\m,&k^*(s)=m.\end{cases}

Each donek(s) is binary and each φk(s) lies in [0, 1). The final predicate is the task verifier. Once all m subgoals are complete, Φ(s) = m.

Shape the value of an action chunk

For a nonterminal chunk, potential-based shaping telescopes to two endpoints. There is no need to evaluate the potential at every intermediate step.

Chunk-endpoint shaping · Eq. (3)
∑i=0h−1γir~t+i=∑i=0h−1γirt+i+βγhΦ(st+h)−βΦ(st)\sum_{i=0}^{h-1}\gamma^i\tilde r_{t+i}=\sum_{i=0}^{h-1}\gamma^i r_{t+i}+\beta\gamma^h\Phi(s_{t+h})-\beta\Phi(s_t)

The critic learns from these shaped returns and supplies advantages for weighted policy updates. At successful completion, both the endpoint-potential term and critic bootstrap are omitted. The −βΦ(st) term remains.

With a fixed, bounded potential and appropriate terminal handling, exact action and state values shift by −βΦ(s), preserving advantages. At deployment, the policy acts without the potential or value critic.
